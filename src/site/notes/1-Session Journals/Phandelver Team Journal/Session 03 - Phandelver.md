# **Session 3: Secrets Beneath**

### **The Morning After**

![Verren 1.png\|left\|200](/img/user/z_Assets/Verren%201.png)Our adventure resumed at Nissan’s unfinished workshop, where the party was interrogating Verren, the Redbrand leader captured after the previous day’s showdown. Through a combination of questioning, threats, and less-than-gentle persuasion, they managed to extract some useful information about the Redbrands and their headquarters beneath **Tresendar Manor**. Eventually, however, Verren lost consciousness, bringing the interrogation to an abrupt end. The party tied him up and left him in the workshop, deciding they had learned enough for now.

Outside, they found Sister Garaele struggling to lift one of the dead Redbrands into a wheelbarrow. She was considerably less impressed with their victory than the townsfolk might have been, sternly reminding them that they couldn’t simply leave corpses lying in the streets. If they were going to kill people, they could at least take responsibility for the bodies and bring them to the Shrine of Luck so she could prepare them for burial.

The party reluctantly agreed, although they first persuaded Garaele to let them keep the dead Redbrands’ distinctive red cloaks.

![Harbin.png\|right](/img/user/z_Assets/Harbin.png)Their cleanup attracted the attention of Townmaster Harbin Wester, who emerged to chastise the adventurers for bringing violence into the streets of Phandalin. The party insisted they had acted in self-defense, but Harbin was having none of it. Fortunately, Sildar arrived and came to their defense, dismissing the indignant townmaster.

Before leaving, Sildar had a question for Rowan. Could he remember precisely when his tribe had been massacred?

Rowan explained that he remembered **Greengrass of 1480 DR** because Elder Tharak had celebrated his eightieth birthday that year. Everyone had considered the coincidence a sign of good fortune, making the tragedy that followed all the more unforgettable.

The following morning, Sildar presented Rowan with a troubling discovery from the town records. An official death notice established that **Lord Alaric Hawthorne had died in 1479 DR**, the year _before_ Rowan remembered the noble ordering the massacre of his people.

Sildar suggested that Rowan’s childhood memories might be mistaken.

But Rowan remembered that Greengrass celebration.

### **A Return to the Redbrands**

Before the party departed, Molly appeared, still bandaged from Rusk’s brutal attack. She had another message from the mysterious woman in black: the woman was still interested in trading for the puzzle box, and she wanted an answer soon.

The party had other priorities.

Armed with the information extracted from Verren, they made their way to the ruined Tresendar Manor. There they discovered a staircase descending into the cellars beneath the old estate.

They had barely begun exploring when three Redbrands attacked.

![Pasted image 20261106120526.png\|center\|400](/img/user/z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261106120526.png)

During the fight, one of the ruffians managed to force Nissan’s head beneath the water of a cistern. The maneuver nearly proved disastrous for the rogue, but it also led to an unexpected discovery: hidden beneath the water was a stash containing supplies, valuables, and other useful items. It looked suspiciously like an emergency escape kit prepared by someone expecting to flee in a hurry.

As the battle turned against the Redbrands, the last surviving thug made a desperate escape through a concealed door.

Moments later, the party heard a terrible shriek.

When they investigated, they caught a glimpse of the Redbrand’s corpse being dragged into a dark crevasse by something lurking below.

Suddenly, the other doors seemed much more inviting.

### **The Dead and the Imprisoned**

One passage led into the ancient Tresendar family crypt, where three skeletons stood guard over the family’s sarcophagi.

The undead guardians began to stir as the party approached, apparently preparing to attack. But then they noticed the red cloaks the adventurers had taken from the fallen Redbrands.

The skeletons stopped.

Whatever enchantment guarded the crypt apparently recognized the cloaks, allowing the party to pass unharmed.

Beyond the crypt, two more Redbrands guarded a small jail. These proved little trouble for the adventurers, who quickly dispatched them.

Nissan picked the lock to one of the cells, where the party discovered the body of **Phandalin’s missing sheriff**.

He was dead.

The sheriff did not appear to have been killed outright. His condition suggested prolonged mistreatment, untreated wounds, and neglect. Whatever the Redbrands had wanted from him, they had left him to die slowly in captivity.

The discovery confirmed just how far the gang’s cruelty extended beyond the streets of Phandalin.

Continuing their exploration, the party discovered an armory filled with weapons and equipment. Unfortunately, the door was locked, and they couldn’t get it open. Rather than abandon the supplies entirely, they used Mage Hand to retrieve whatever weapons they could reach.

Their search also revealed another secret passage leading toward the same ominous crevasse where the fleeing Redbrand had met his end.

This time, they decided to investigate.

### **The Eye in the Darkness**

![Pasted image 20261106121001.png\|left\|240](/img/user/z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261106121001.png)Deep beneath the manor, the party encountered one of the strangest creatures they had ever seen.

A twisted, monstrous being with an enormous green eye emerged from the darkness. It was a **Nothic** calling himself **Ixal**, a creature capable of reading their thoughts and speaking directly into their minds.

The Nothic seemed half-mad, alternating between unsettling observations and offers of cooperation. Despite its terrifying appearance, it was surprisingly willing to negotiate.

Its terms were simple: keep it supplied with bodies, and it would allow the adventurers to live.

The party entertained the arrangement, and the creature proved remarkably knowledgeable about the hideout. It knew where the remaining Redbrands were stationed and even offered advice on how the adventurers might catch them by surprise.

With this information, the group devised a plan to lure the remaining Redbrands into an ambush.

Unfortunately, plans rarely survive contact with the enemy.

### **Glasstaff!**

![Glasstaff.png\|right](/img/user/z_Assets/Glasstaff.png)The party split up and attempted to draw the Redbrands into a fight. Although the adventurers suffered several painful blows, they gradually gained the upper hand until only a single Redbrand remained standing.

Then a figure appeared across the hall.

The surviving ruffian looked up, his face suddenly filled with relief.

**“Glasstaff! Thank the gods!”**

For a brief moment, he believed his leader had come to save him.

But Glasstaff was already reading from a magical scroll.

The Redbrand’s relief turned to horror as he realized what was about to happen.

A deafening magical blast tore through the room. Furniture splintered, debris exploded outward, and the unfortunate Redbrand was obliterated by his own leader’s spell. The adventurers were caught in the blast as well, suffering heavy damage.

Through the settling dust, Glasstaff turned and fled.

The party had only moments to decide whether to tend to their wounds or pursue the wizard before he escaped.

They chose pursuit.

### **Where Things Stand**

The Redbrand hideout has been largely cleared, but its master is still at large. Glasstaff has demonstrated both his magical power and his complete disregard for the lives of his own followers. The party is now racing through the tunnels beneath Tresendar Manor, hoping to catch him before he disappears.

Meanwhile, the discovery of the sheriff’s body confirms the Redbrands’ brutality, and the strange Nothic remains beneath the manor, apparently willing to bargain with anyone who can satisfy its appetite.

Back in Phandalin, Verren remains bound in Nissan’s workshop, Bart is still searching for the missing Rockseeker brothers, and the mysterious woman in black is growing impatient for an answer about the puzzle box.

And Sildar’s investigation has produced its first unsettling contradiction: Rowan remembers Lord Hawthorne ordering the massacre of his tribe in 1480, but official records say the man died in 1479.

For now, however, there is no time to unravel that mystery.

**Glasstaff is getting away.**
